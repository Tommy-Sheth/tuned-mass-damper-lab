Abstract

This page runs the model from our report: the first sway mode of Taipei 101 as one mass on a spring, with the 660 t pendulum hanging inside it. Pick a load and press Play. The controls change the building and the damper, and every plot and number on the page follows them.

3The model

The building’s first sway mode is one mass 𝑀 on a spring 𝑘 and a dashpot 𝑐, all referred to the damper floor. The pendulum is a mass 𝑚 on the spring 𝑘𝑑=𝑚𝑔/𝐿 that gravity provides for small swings, with a dashpot 𝑐𝑑=2𝜁𝑑𝑚𝜔𝑑 standing in for the hydraulic dampers. With 𝑥 and 𝑦 the motions of the floor and of the sphere relative to the ground,

𝑀𝑥¨+𝑐𝑥˙+𝑘𝑥 −𝑐𝑑(𝑦˙−𝑥˙) −𝑘𝑑(𝑦−𝑥) =𝐹(𝑡)−Γ𝑀𝑎𝑔(𝑡), (1)
𝑚𝑦¨+𝑐𝑑(𝑦˙−𝑥˙) +𝑘𝑑(𝑦−𝑥) =−𝑚𝑎𝑔(𝑡), (2)

where 𝐹 is the wind or the push and 𝑎𝑔 the ground acceleration in an earthquake, with Γ=1.247 for the first mode of a uniform shear beam. The page solves (1) and (2) exactly over each time step, as our Python code does.

The published inputs are the damper mass and its 1.5 m bumper ring [1], the first-mode frequency of 0.150 Hz [2] and the modal mass of 39,655 t [3]. The building’s own damping of 1.5% and Γ are our assumptions. Not modelled: higher modes, the real dampers’ 𝐹=𝐶𝑣2 law, the bumper ring itself, torsion, and Taipei 101’s wind-tunnel data, which is not public.
